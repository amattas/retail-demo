Live event contract¶

Runtime¶

stream-events.ipynb is rendered from utility/notebooks/templates/driver-05-stream.py. It is an optional, long-running Fabric Spark Structured Streaming driver.

Supported sinks:

  • eventhouse: direct typed writes through the Spark Kusto connector;
  • delta: development/validation landing table.

The current path does not require Kafka, Event Hubs, or a Fabric Eventstream.

Before emitting events, the driver requires readable, nonempty Silver dim_stores, dim_customers, dim_products, dim_distribution_centers, and dim_trucks. Product/store attribute reads provide prices and tax data. Missing/empty dimensions, a store without a tax_rate, or failed attribute reads stop startup rather than substituting arbitrary ID ranges or synthetic attribute frames. Complete historical setup before starting the stream.

Foreign keys come only from IDs that exist. The driver numbers each dimension's IDs and picks one per row with a seeded hash, through a broadcast join, so gaps in the IDs (a custom layout or a filtered publish) are safe. Baskets, and therefore inventory, stockout and reorder events, use only products whose LaunchDate is on or before the checkpoint's catalog date. A dimension with contiguous IDs 1..n gets the same IDs as the earlier 1..count draw.

History handoff¶

History ends yesterday in store-local time, so the newest Silver fact_receipts.event_ts is normally less than a day before the stream starts. At startup the driver compares the two (both UTC):

Condition New checkpoint Resumed checkpoint
Gap of one day or less Starts Starts
History ends up to 14 hours after the start (store time zones) Starts, logs the overlap Starts
Gap over one day, overlap over 14 hours, or no receipts Fails Warns

Set the notebook parameter allow_history_gap = True to start a new stream anyway. A resumed checkpoint only warns, because its own downtime is an expected gap (see the catch-up cap below).

Time and shared vocabulary¶

Every timestamp the stream writes is UTC; the stream never assumes store-local time. Historical Silver uses the same convention (see the historical data contract), and fact_receipts.local_hour gives the store-local hour for both producers.

Tenders, zones, ID formats, marketing channels and devices, promotions, and the decline model come from retail_setup.generation.vocabulary, which the build inlines into the notebook, so live values match history: MOBILE_PAY, base-36 BLE IDs padded to six characters, BEACON_%03d_<zone> and SENSOR_%03d_<zone> IDs, upper-case devices, CAMP<yyyyMMdd><NN> campaign IDs, and padded AdIds. Online orders use the same fulfillment mix, online tenders, decline model, cancellation and backorder rates, promo codes and stage delays (vocabulary.ONLINE_*) as history. No known gap is left; new ones are listed in utility/tests/generation/test_vocabulary.py, which fails when a listed gap closes, so the list only shrinks.

Volume, hours and customers¶

The live stream reuses the historical models (stream_calendar, stream_events), so its volume, time of day and customer mix continue the history:

  • Rate. source_rows_per_second = 0 (the default) derives the rate-source rate from the store count, the historical transactions_per_store_day (rendered into the notebook), online_orders_per_day, and the marketing volume: the smallest whole rate that covers every lane at its peak. Each row goes to one lane (store visits, online orders, marketing) and is kept with that lane's calendar weight, so a larger explicit rate adds resolution, not volume. volume_scale multiplies every calibrated volume. Inventory and truck events have no lane: they follow from the purchases (below).
  • Store visits. A visit picks a store weighted by daily_traffic_multiplier and is kept with the profile's hourly, daily and monthly weights evaluated in the store's local time (dim_stores.TimeZone, daylight saving included). The hourly weight is zero while the store is closed and on Christmas Day, so no live receipt is outside opening hours. Visits are drawn at transactions_per_store_day / 0.225 per store-day and convert with the same store/day/hour conversion as history (about 22%).
  • Customers. As in history (generation/customers.py), about 45% of visits are anonymous. The rest pick a customer from the store's home-customer pool (85%) or another store's, weighted by visit tickets, and keep them only while they are signed up and not churned on the visit's local day; live churn continues from the history's lifecycle (rendered start, end, churn rate and anonymous share). Online orders take the first of four tries that is active that day.
  • Visit path. A visit enters (customer_entered, sensor zone by store format), walks from the store type's entrance zone through one to three of its department zones (profile.zones, upper-cased) in random order, passes CHECKOUT when it buys and leaves through EXIT when the store type has one. Each zone has a dwell time in its foot-traffic range, two to five BLE pings, and a zone change on arrival. 30% of visits carry the customer's dim_customers.BLEId; the rest are ANON-<store>-<n>, as in history. The receipt, lines and promotions are emitted halfway through checkout and the payment after the tender's processing time. A visit that would run past closing time is compressed to end before it.
  • Purchases. Baskets (complementary products and merged repeated SKUs, generation/baskets.py), shelf prices of the incarnation's catalog date (generation/pricing.py, the same price schedule as history), one basket-level promotion priced over the lines the store could fill, the 40/30/20/10 tender mix and the 2.5% base decline model are the batch helpers. A declined payment keeps its SALE receipt, as in history, and is never attributed. One promotion_applied is emitted per receipt and promo code, so at most one per receipt. Visit and online volume follow history's year-over-year trend (annual_volume_growth from the anchor date).
  • Store operations. store_opened and store_closed (lower-case opened/closed, like history) are emitted exactly at each store's local opening and closing time, one pair per open day. A 24-hour store closes at 23:59 and reopens at midnight.
  • Marketing. Background impressions follow the historical campaigns (archetype channels, CAMP IDs on the same campaign windows, flash sales on about one day in seven), cost bands by channel and device, padded AdIds, and a 5% CRM match, at store_count / 86 of the archetype volumes.

Store inventory¶

stream_supply keeps a running on-hand balance per store and product and replays, in time order, everything that changes it in a micro-batch:

  • Sales. Every receipt line, and every line of a ship-from-store or BOPIS online order (at order time), takes its quantity from the store: inventory_updated with reason = SALE, source = CUSTOMER_PURCHASE or ONLINE_ORDER, a negative quantity_delta and the demand in requested_quantity. Ship-from-DC lines draw on the DC, which, as in history, is an unconstrained supplier.
  • Lost sales. A line the store cannot fill whole becomes reason = LOST_SALE with quantity_delta = 0, like history's policy: the line is left off its receipt or order (and its totals), and a purchase with no line left emits nothing. Balances never go negative.
  • Stockouts. A sale that takes the balance below calibration.STOCKOUT_QUANTITY_THRESHOLD (to zero) emits stockout_detected with the stock it took (last_known_quantity), once per store, product and UTC day, the crossing fact_stockouts records in history.
  • Reorders. After each sale or lost sale the shared replenishment policy (inventory_policy: smoothed daily demand, reorder point of 4 days, order-up-to target of 8 days, 12-unit case pack, URGENT when the shelf is empty, HIGH below two days of demand) compares the position (on hand plus inbound) with the reorder point and emits reorder_triggered.
  • Receipts of stock. A truck unload adds its units: reason = INBOUND_SHIPMENT, source = the shipment ID.

Every inventory, stockout and reorder event carries the store's DC (dc_id), the nearest DC by geography, as in the historical inventory chain (network.store_dc_map). A store and product the stream has not touched yet starts from its latest Silver fact_store_inventory_txn balance as of the incarnation start (never below zero) and the mean daily demand of the 7 days before it; one Silver has never seen starts from history's opening stock. The seed time is kept in the meta state, so a restart seeds the same way. History's shipments that were still on the road when history ended are not carried over; the policy reorders that stock.

Trucks¶

A reorder joins its store's pending load at the DC. Each store takes one consolidated delivery a day: at the store's daily delivery slot (a stable minute of the UTC day, so the slots spread over the day), the next free truck of the DC's fleet (dim_trucks.DCID; pool trucks only for a DC with none) loads the whole pending load for TRUCK_LOAD_MINUTES, drives the lane (a stable 1-4 hour draw per DC and store), and arrives. Each DC's fleet is sized to the stores it serves (network.TRUCKS_PER_STORE, at least two trucks), so a typical fleet is busy about 70% of the day. planned_arrival_time is set at dispatch; the actual arrival is the planned time plus delay_minutes, which stays 0 with no delay_reason until the logistics incident model adds delays (SupplyBatch.held_dcs already lets an incident stop a DC dispatching). The unload takes TRUCK_UNLOAD_BASE_MINUTES plus the load at TRUCK_UNLOAD_UNITS_PER_MINUTE, scaled by a per-store dock factor, with a few minutes of noise (estimated_unload_duration is the plan, the departure's actual_unload_duration the outcome). A typical load unloads inside the normal 30-75 minute range; an unusually large one can breach the 90-minute SLA. The truck departs when the unload is done, which restocks the store, and drives back. A truck carries one shipment at a time, so it is never in two places at once, and truck_arrived and truck_departed are emitted only once their time has passed.

Online orders¶

Online orders arrive at online_orders_per_day (by default derived so online orders are the profile's online_order_share of all orders), shaped by the profile's monthly weights and the online hourly curve (vocabulary.ONLINE_HOURLY_WEIGHTS, UTC, peaking in the US evening; history uses the same curve). Each order has one fulfillment mode and node, drawn by the historical mix (60% ship from a DC, 30% ship from a store, 10% BOPIS). Its lifecycle runs over hours and days:

Event When
online_order_created and one online_order_line_added per line order time
payment_processed (order_id, no store) after the online tender's processing time
online_order_picked 30-240 minutes later (BOPIS: 4-24 hours, ready for pickup)
online_order_shipped 2-4 hours after picking; never for BOPIS
online_order_delivered 1-3 days after shipping; BOPIS: 1-24 hours after picking, when the customer collects it

2% of orders are cancelled at once: zero totals, no payment and no fulfillment. 8% of shippable orders are backordered: shipped but not delivered. Payments use the online tenders (no cash) and decline like history; a declined order is still fulfilled, as in history. Lines carry history's online promo codes, and tax is the fulfilling store's rate for store-fulfilled orders and the customer's destination rate (mean store rate of their geography) for DC shipments. Orders still in progress live in the order state.

Stream state¶

Events that span micro-batches need state. A visit's and an online order's later events are emitted in the batch whose time they fall in, the store hours need each store's open/closed state, and stock and trucks carry over between batches. The state lives in Lakehouse Delta tables in the Silver schema:

Table Key Contents
_stream_state_stores store ID Open or closed, and when it last changed.
_stream_state_visits visit (rate row) Visits whose last event is still in the future, with their lost-sale lines.
_stream_state_orders order (rate row) Online orders whose last stage is still in the future, with their lost-sale lines.
_stream_state_inventory inv:<store>:<product> On hand, inbound, smoothed daily demand and the last stockout day of each store and product the stream has touched.
_stream_state_logistics pending:<store>, shipment:<id>, truck:<id> Loads waiting at the DC, shipments on the road and where each truck is.
_stream_state_meta clock, seed The time of the last micro-batch, and the inventory seed time.

Each row also carries the checkpoint incarnation (stream ID and nonce), batch_id, the JSON payload and a deleted flag. Micro-batch N reads, per entity, the latest version written before N, and writes its changes with batch_id = N using a Delta replaceWhere on batch_id >= N. A replay of N therefore reads the same state, regenerates the same events, and replaces its own versions: nothing is applied twice. Store inventory is read only for the store-products a batch touches. Superseded versions are deleted every 60 batches. A new incarnation has no rows; at startup it seeds each store from its last Silver fact_store_ops row (or its hours) as batch_id = -1. Rows of an earlier incarnation are ignored and can be deleted.

Envelope¶

Every event contains:

  • event_type
  • trace_id
  • ingest_timestamp
  • schema_version
  • source
  • correlation_id
  • partition_key
  • session_id
  • parent_event_id

Payload fields are event-specific and mapped by EVENT_PAYLOADS.

Business event types¶

  1. receipt_created
  2. receipt_line_added
  3. payment_processed
  4. inventory_updated
  5. stockout_detected
  6. reorder_triggered
  7. customer_entered
  8. customer_zone_changed
  9. ble_ping_detected
  10. truck_arrived
  11. truck_departed
  12. store_opened
  13. store_closed
  14. ad_impression
  15. promotion_applied
  16. online_order_created
  17. online_order_picked
  18. online_order_shipped
  19. online_order_line_added
  20. online_order_delivered
  21. logistics_incident_started (declared, not yet emitted)
  22. logistics_incident_resolved (declared, not yet emitted)

Types 21-22 are declared end to end (KQL table, mapping, EVENT_PAYLOADS, Silver route, contract path and fixture) so the logistics-incident model can start emitting them without another contract change. The stream emits the first twenty today.

  • logistics_incident_started carries incident_id, incident_type (DC_OUTAGE, LANE_DELAY, WEATHER, HOLIDAY, SUPPLIER_SHORT_SHIP), dc_id, store_id (null for DC- or network-wide incidents), started_at, severity, affected_store_count, and description. logistics_incident_resolved carries the same identity and resolved_at. Silver keeps one fact_logistics_incidents row per incident: the start inserts it and the resolution sets resolved_at once (Delta MERGE). A resolution whose start has aged out of Bronze only updates an existing row.
  • online_order_line_added carries the order line (line_number, product_id, quantity, prices, promo_code) and its fulfillment node; it lands in fact_online_order_lines with status PENDING. Each Silver run then moves recent live lines with their order: PICKED, SHIPPED, DELIVERED (with the stage timestamps), or CANCELLED when the order's header has zero totals.
  • online_order_delivered adds a DELIVERED row to the streaming-only fact_online_order_status table, like picked and shipped.
  • inventory_updated.requested_quantity is the customer's demand of a SALE or LOST_SALE (0 for stock receipts). Silver writes it to requested_quantity and, for a lost sale, to unfulfilled_quantity. The KQL script restates the table with .alter table so the column sits before the envelope, as the positional writes need.
  • A live store stockout keeps DCID NULL in Silver fact_stockouts, as history does; the event's dc_id names the store's DC.

KQL defines an additional unknown_event catch-all table. It is not a generated business event. The current notebook rejects unmapped event types before committing the micro-batch checkpoint. The catch-all is an operational queued-ingestion boundary, not an active direct-writer DLQ. Here DLQ means dead-letter queue: a place to preserve messages that could not be processed.

Eventhouse writes¶

Each micro-batch is generated inside foreachBatch: the notebook reads the state as of the previous batch, builds the batch's events, writes them, and only then commits the new state. For the Eventhouse sink, the notebook:

  1. Persists the batch.
  2. Finds present mapped event types.
  3. Resolves one notebook runtime token.
  4. Maps JSON to typed envelope/payload columns.
  5. Writes event types concurrently to their same-named KQL tables, one coalesced partition (one blob, one extent, one tag) per table.
  6. Uses FailIfNotExist.
  7. Sets flushImmediately=true.
  8. Unpersists the batch.

Each table write retries transient errors (throttling, HTTP 401/408/429/5xx, timeouts, dropped connections, an expired token) up to five attempts with exponential backoff (2, 4, 8, 16 seconds) and a fresh token. Permanent errors, such as a missing table or a type mismatch, are not retried.

If kusto_uri is blank, the notebook resolves the KQL database queryServiceUri by display name in the current workspace.

The event tables carry an extent_tags_retention policy that drops ingest-by: tags after seven days. They set no streaming-ingestion or ingestion-batching policy: the connector's transactional writes are queued ingestion with flushImmediately, which bypasses batching, and nothing streams into these tables. Eventhouses created by earlier versions keep those policies; they are inert. The unknown_event catch-all keeps both for queued ingestion.

Trigger, catch-up and restarts¶

Parameter Default Meaning
source_rows_per_second 0 0 derives the rate from the store count and historical volume; a larger value adds resolution, not volume.
volume_scale 1.0 Multiplies every calibrated live volume.
run_seconds 0 0 runs until cancelled; a positive value is a bounded (smoke) run.
trigger_seconds 60 Live micro-batch interval. Bounded runs use at most 10 seconds.
max_lag_seconds 600 After a stop, rate rows older than this behind the batch time are dropped.
max_restarts 5 Supervised restarts of a failed live query.
allow_history_gap False Start a new stream despite a history gap or overlap.

Spark's rate source emits every second of downtime in the next micro-batch; a 12-hour stop used to produce about a million events in one batch. The driver keeps only rows within max_lag_seconds of the batch time, so a resumed stream continues from about now and leaves a gap for the downtime. The batch time comes from Spark's offset log, so a replayed batch keeps exactly the same rows. The newer rate-micro-batch source was not used: its timestamps advance per batch, not with the clock, so after a stop every event would stay backdated.

A live query that fails with a transient error restarts from its checkpoint after 30, 60, 120 ... seconds (at most 10 minutes), up to max_restarts times. The count resets after an hour of healthy running. A permanent failure, or one beyond the budget, ends the notebook with the error.

Checkpoints and incarnations¶

Checkpoints live under Files/setup/stream/checkpoint:

Path Contents
_metadata/stream_id The logical stream ID, shared by every sink checkpoint.
eventhouse-live/, delta-live/ Spark checkpoint of an unbounded run.
eventhouse-smoke/, delta-smoke/ Spark checkpoint of a bounded run, so smoke tests never share offsets with the live stream.
_metadata/<checkpoint>.json The checkpoint's incarnation record: an 8-hex nonce and the catalog date.

Earlier versions used eventhouse/ and delta/; they are no longer read and can be deleted.

The incarnation nonce is part of every ingest tag (retail-demo:<stream_id>:<incarnation>:<event_type>:<batch_id>), trace ID, and business ID (RCP-<stream_id>-<incarnation>-<n>). Spark restarts batch IDs at 0 when its checkpoint is deleted. Without the nonce, those batches would reuse tags Kusto still holds, and the connector would silently skip the writes.

At startup the driver compares the record with the Spark offsets and commits logs:

State Result
No record, no Spark log New incarnation; the record is written just before the query starts.
Record and offsets, last batch committed Resume; the catalog date moves to today, so new launches are sold.
Record and offsets, last batch not committed Resume; the pending batch replays with the recorded catalog date, so it regenerates the same baskets, IDs, and tags.
Record without offsets Refused: the Spark checkpoint was reset or never ran a batch.
Offsets without a record, commits past the offsets, or a record for another stream Refused.

The error names the files to delete. To start a new incarnation, delete the Spark checkpoint folder and its record together. To start a new stream ID, delete the whole checkpoint root.

Duplicate and failure behavior¶

  • Generated business IDs and trace_id include the persisted stream ID and the checkpoint incarnation.
  • Eventhouse writes use the connector's transactional mode, deterministic request IDs, duplicate-blob protection, and matching ingest-by/check tags for each stream, incarnation, event type, and Spark micro-batch.
  • Unmapped event types and any required table-write failure (after retries) raise from foreachBatch, so Spark does not commit that batch's checkpoint.
  • A replayed batch rebuilds the same rows, catalog, state, and tags, so tables that already succeeded are skipped and the rest are written once. The state write replaces the batch's own versions.
  • Silver transforms remove exact duplicate candidates, reject conflicting rows with the same identity, and use Delta MERGE before advancing watermarks.
  • Gold tables are rebuilt with overwrite semantics from duplicate-safe Silver inputs, so reruns do not accumulate aggregate rows.

These controls make expected retries idempotent and are covered by injected failure/replay contracts. tests/test_streaming_dedup_contract.py executes the actual foreachBatch sink function with mocked connector operations, including partial table-write, transient-retry, unmapped-event, and credential failures. utility/tests/generation/test_stream_control.py covers the backlog cap, retries, supervised restarts, incarnation records over a temporary directory, the history handoff, and ID sampling. These tests do not establish live Spark/Kusto transactional behavior. The readiness adapter checks live ingestion and checkpoint tags. The remaining IMP-013 evidence includes a recent bounded run of the intentionally manual stream and live execution of the bounded receipt/inventory/sales reconciliation query.

Cross-layer ownership¶

The exact KQL table shape is in 01-create-tables.kql. JSON ingestion mappings in 02-create-ingestion-mappings.kql support queued raw-JSON ingestion but are not used by the direct typed live path.

Silver mappings are separately implemented in fabric/lakehouse/03-streaming-to-silver.ipynb. Their current divergence from the historical contract is documented in Fabric analytics.

contracts/retail-demo.json declares one stable path ID per emitted event and one derived attribution path. It stores keys, UTC event-time semantics, targets, terminals, and named exceptions, but never copies physical field or type inventories. python scripts/check_data_contracts.py derives those inventories from the driver, KQL, schemas.py, notebooks, and active TMDL.

Truck lifecycle¶

truck_arrived and truck_departed share truck_id, dc_id, store_id, and shipment_id. The departure is emitted when the unload completes, after the arrival and never ahead of the stream clock; the dwell follows the load-size and dock model under Trucks. Its parameters come from utility/src/retail_setup/generation/calibration.py, the same module the rules, anomaly functions, and semantic model are checked against.

truck_arrived also carries planned_arrival_time, delay_minutes, and delay_reason. Until the logistics incident model lands, live trucks arrive as planned (delay_minutes = 0, no reason). Silver copies them to fact_truck_moves. The KQL script restates the table with .alter table so an existing Eventhouse takes the declared column order, which the positional Spark Kusto writes rely on.

Silver joins the two event tables on all four lifecycle keys and writes one completed fact_truck_moves row. Eventhouse and Gold expose dwell in minutes with STORE_<id> / DC_<id> site labels.

tests/test_truck_dwell_contract.py verifies the generator, rendered stream notebook, KQL table/function contract, Silver join, Gold aggregation, queryset, dashboard template, and 90-minute rule. A live Fabric smoke run remains the deployment verification gate.

Marketing attribution¶

About 5% of approved store and online purchases with a customer are selected for a journey with the batch helper (attribution.with_journey_plan): one journey ID, a product-launch or loyalty campaign, and two ad_impression touches 3-7 days and 1 minute-2 days before the purchase. The existing envelope correlation_id carries attribution_journey_id through impressions, receipt/order creation, promotion, payment, and online status events.

Touches arrive with the purchase: their ingest_timestamp is the purchase time, and the payload's impression_time says when they were shown (background impressions have both equal). Notebook 03 dates fact_marketing rows by impression_time but tracks its watermark and replay window on ingest_timestamp, the arrival time, so touches are never older than the replay window and are not left to the daily deep reconciliation. Kusto's ingestion_time() is not visible through the OneLake shortcut notebook 03 reads; ingest_timestamp stays deterministic from the rate-source time, so replays are identical, and is within one trigger interval of it. KQL fn_marketing_attribution() and fn_campaign_performance() use coalesce(impression_time, ingest_timestamp) as the touch time.

The contract is deterministic last-touch within an inclusive seven-day window, ordered by touch timestamp and impression ID. Silver writes one fact_marketing_attribution row after purchase, promotion, and approved payment reconcile; attributed rows additionally require a valid selected touch, while purchases without a journey are marked UNATTRIBUTED_NO_JOURNEY.

Money remains integer cents:

gross_subtotal_cents - discount_cents = net_subtotal_cents
net_subtotal_cents + tax_cents = total_cents
approved payment_cents = total_cents
attributed_revenue_cents = net_subtotal_cents

Eventhouse exposes reconciliation diagnostics through fn_marketing_attribution(). Consumer-facing fn_attribution() and fn_attribution_window() return only reconciled ATTRIBUTED purchases after future and out-of-window touches are removed and the latest valid touch is selected. fn_campaign_performance() uses that reconciled set. Gold publishes campaign_performance_daily, and the Direct Lake model exposes both the audit fact and campaign KPIs. Contract tests cover batch determinism, live payload/KQL mappings, Silver/Gold wiring, semantic measures, and all cent equations. A live Fabric smoke run remains the deployment verification gate.

Contract evidence and live boundary¶

Eight fixture scenarios cover all 22 declared types plus nullable and unknown-event variants. The parameterized matrix validates the nine-field envelope, KQL DDL/mappings, UTC values, business/dedupe keys, Silver/Gold routes, and either an active semantic terminal or the named streaming-only exception.

Repository acceptance for IMP-005 is complete. A live Fabric staging run through Eventhouse, the optional Silver/Gold projection, and Direct Lake remains required external evidence. The static check does not prove tenant-level execution.
