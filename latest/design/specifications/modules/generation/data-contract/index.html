Historical data contract¶

Authority¶

utility/src/retail_setup/generation/schemas.py defines the base Lakehouse tables, columns, and types. engine.py orchestrates generation, invariants.py validates relationships across tables, writer.py publishes outputs, and gold.py builds the ten aggregates from gold_rules.py, the one Gold definition that notebook 04 also uses.

Base Silver output¶

Dimensions¶

dim_geographies, dim_stores, dim_distribution_centers, dim_trucks, dim_customers, dim_products, and dim_date.

dim_date uses ISO 8601 weeks: day_of_week runs Monday (1) to Sunday (7), and week_of_year is the ISO week (1-53; week 1 holds the year's first Thursday), so the first days of January can belong to week 52 or 53 and the last days of December to week 1. year stays the calendar year. Fiscal years run July to June and fiscal_year is labelled by the calendar year they start in: July 2025 to June 2026 is fiscal year 2025, and July is fiscal_quarter 1.

dim_products prices and names:

  • A base product sold by several brands keeps one title per brand: a title that starts with a brand name carries the SKU's own Brand; generic titles stay generic.
  • MSRP is the base price +/-15% and SalePrice equals it or a 5-35% discount from it, both at the nearest charm price (.49 or .99, a 9-cent ending below $1). Catalog base prices (dictionaries/catalog.py) take the nearest charm price inside their subcategory band, so sub-dollar and narrow bands keep their spread.
  • Cost leaves the department's gross margin band (pricing.DEPARTMENT_MARGINS, for example groceries 15-30%, electronics 10-25%, apparel 45-60%, jewelry 45-65%).

Facts¶

fact_receipts, fact_receipt_lines, fact_payments, fact_store_ops, fact_foot_traffic, fact_ble_pings, fact_customer_zone_changes, fact_marketing, fact_marketing_attribution, fact_promotions, fact_promo_lines, fact_online_order_headers, fact_online_order_lines, fact_reorders, fact_truck_moves, fact_truck_inventory, fact_dc_inventory_txn, fact_store_inventory_txn, fact_stockouts, fact_logistics_incidents, and fact_logistics_incident_stores.

Each store is served by a DC in its own state, else its region, else any DC; among those the store goes to the DC serving the fewest stores so far, so DCs in one state share the load (network.assign_dcs). Geographies are sampled one city per state in turn, so the shared dictionary's skew (most cities in five states) does not concentrate stores and DCs. dim_trucks gives each DC one truck per daily delivery route (network.plan_routes, at least two trucks), plus a shared pool of about 15% of the fleet with a NULL DCID. A route is a set of the DC's stores in one time zone, each with a daily delivery slot in store-local time; slots on a route are spaced by the longest possible trip (load, the lane both ways, the maximum unload, a turnaround), so a truck on its route is never in two places at once. History and the live stream use the same routes and slots.

Online orders follow vocabulary.ONLINE_HOURLY_WEIGHTS over the UTC day (peaking in the US evening), and a BOPIS order is collected 1-24 hours after it is picked (delivered_ts); the stream uses the same parameters. Online orders are local, like the live stream (stream_events.plan_orders):

  • The customer is active on the order day and comes from a home-store pool drawn by store traffic, so an order belongs to the customer's home store.
  • One fulfillment mode serves the whole order (60% ship-from-DC, 30% ship-from-store, 10% BOPIS). Ship-from-store and BOPIS orders use the customer's home store as node_id; ship-from-DC orders use the DC that supplies that store.
  • Baskets are sized to the store type's avg_ticket_target like store receipts, lines follow the same shopping missions, and repeated SKUs merge into one line.
  • A cancelled or voided order has zero header totals and zero line ext_cents, and no payment.
  • 8% of shipped orders are backordered: they wait 3-14 days for stock between picking and shipping, then ship and deliver.
  • fulfillment_status and the stage timestamps are as of the end of history. Orders still in fulfillment then are PENDING, PICKED, or SHIPPED, with NULL timestamps for the stages still to come; no stage time is later than the end of history. The live stream does not continue these orders.

fact_logistics_incidents holds one row per logistics disruption (incident_type is DC_OUTAGE, LANE_DELAY, WEATHER, HOLIDAY_CAPACITY, or SUPPLIER_SHORT_SHIP), with resolved_at null while the incident is open at the end of history and store_id set only for a lane delay's store. fact_logistics_incident_stores lists every store each incident affects (the DC's stores, the lane's store, or a geography's stores), one row per incident and store. History and the live stream plan incidents with the same model (see Logistics incidents). fact_truck_moves carries planned_arrival_time, delay_minutes, delay_reason, and load_units.

Time convention¶

Generation runs in each store's local wall-clock time: opening hours, the hourly traffic curve, store operations, and the inventory schedule are local. Silver stores true UTC, the same convention as the live stream:

  • dim_stores.TimeZone is the store's IANA zone, from its geography's state (generation/timezones.py, STATE_TIME_ZONES).
  • Every published timestamp column is UTC, and event_date is the UTC date of event_ts. An evening sale on the West Coast can therefore carry the next day's event_date.
  • Store events convert with their store's zone; receipt and promotion lines use the store encoded in receipt_id_ext. A shipment's whole lifecycle (truck moves, truck loads and unloads, and the DC rows that mirror the loads) uses the destination store's zone, so one shipment never mixes zones and DC balances follow UTC order.
  • Online orders and marketing impressions are not tied to store hours. They are generated directly in UTC and not converted. Attribution runs on UTC purchases.
  • fact_receipts.local_hour is the store-local hour (0-23) for hour-of-day reporting. Notebook 03 sets it for streamed receipts from dim_stores.TimeZone.

Invariants that depend on the local clock (operating hours) convert back with the store's zone. Existing environments must re-run setup after this change; Silver written before it has local timestamps and no TimeZone or local_hour.

Shared vocabulary and calibration¶

generation/vocabulary.py owns the names and formats both producers use: tenders and the decline model, the promotion catalog and per-line eligibility, BLE, ad, beacon, sensor, and campaign ID formats, zones, marketing channels and cost bands, device types, and fulfillment modes. generation/calibration.py owns thresholds that consumers repeat as literals: the 90-minute truck dwell SLA, stockout and reorder thresholds, the zone-surge count (sized from the stream's own visit rate at the store type's peak), the high-value receipt rule (10 x the store type's avg_ticket_target, about one ticket in a thousand), and the anomaly detector parameters. Contract tests fail when the stream, rules, KQL functions, or semantic model drift from them. The two store-type thresholds reach the rules through the Eventhouse function fn_rule_thresholds(), whose values the deployment's KQL step writes for the deployed store type (calibration.rule_thresholds).

Operational output¶

setup_run_log appends a unique setup-attempt record, table-level completion records, and a final completion or failure record. Reusing an existing run_id is rejected so retries cannot create ambiguous duplicate history.

Setup-02 generates and validates dimensions without publishing them. Setup-03 through setup-09 regenerate the same deterministic dimensions and generate the facts as handoff tables. Setup-10 (setup-10-publish-silver) is the single Silver publication boundary. write_all stages every candidate under a run-scoped <schema>_stage schema, validates schema and row counts, captures existing Delta versions, then promotes. An attempted promotion failure restores pre-existing targets and drops newly created targets in reverse order.

Each Silver run's setup_run_log rows carry the generation_fingerprint of the configuration and dictionaries it published. Setup-10 drops the handoffs after a successful publication, so a retry (for example a pipeline activity retry after a timeout) finds none. When the latest completed Silver publication carries the same fingerprint and its tables still exist, the retry succeeds without publishing again; otherwise it fails on the missing handoffs and the generation notebooks must run first.

Publication is atomic for writers, not for readers. The Silver tables are promoted concurrently, each with its own CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE, so a query running during promotion can read some tables from the new generation and others from the old one. A failed promotion rolls every table back. Gold is built afterwards by setup-11 (setup-11-build-gold, in the ml-required pipeline); until it finishes, or if it fails, Gold describes the previous Silver. Consumers should not read during setup and should treat Gold as current only once setup-11 has completed for the same run.

Terminal publication states distinguish data recovery from staging cleanup: COMPLETED, FAILED, ROLLED_BACK, ROLLBACK_FAILED, COMPLETED_CLEANUP_FAILED, and ROLLED_BACK_CLEANUP_FAILED.

COMPLETED_CLEANUP_FAILED means data promotion succeeded: the writer returns success with a warning and logs retained staging artifacts. Readiness reports cleanup as degradation, not failed historical data. Rollback states still represent failed publication attempts even when restoration succeeded.

Historical Silver replacement is blocked once the configured <lakehouse>.<silver_db>._watermarks table exists, even if it has no rows. Stop writers and use coordinated Silver/checkpoint recovery before rebuilding history; deleting watermarks alone would allow replay to corrupt rebuilt data. The writer guard is not a distributed lock, so operators must serialize historical and streaming writers.

The retained historical loader must materialize the exact ordered TABLES field names and types, not sampled/inferred Parquet schemas. Synthetic fixture data must not silently stand in for a missing or incompatible historical source.

Gold output¶

  • sales_minute_store
  • top_products_15m
  • inventory_position_current
  • dc_inventory_position_current
  • truck_dwell_daily
  • online_sales_daily
  • zone_dwell_minute
  • marketing_cost_daily
  • campaign_performance_daily
  • tender_mix_daily

ML output tables are not part of this base contract.

Every Gold table is built from gold_rules.py: sales tables count SALE receipts with an approved payment, online sales count orders with an approved payment, truck_dwell_daily has one row per completed shipment lifecycle, and no source row at or after the Gold cutoff is used (see Fabric analytics).

The source-derived live-route check compares this inventory with declared event paths. Seven dimensions, fact_promo_lines, fact_truck_inventory, and dc_inventory_position_current are the named historical-only boundary. fact_dc_inventory_txn has a live route (the stream emits DC stock moves as inventory_updated rows without a store), as do fact_online_order_lines, fact_logistics_incidents and fact_logistics_incident_stores. The manifest records those targets and rationale; schemas.py continues to own their fields, types, nullability behavior, and mixed-case compatibility.

Generation order¶

The engine creates dimensions and date context, then candidate store demand. It resolves inventory-constrained fulfillment before publishing sales, returns, payments, promotions, attribution, sensors, and stock ledgers. Online orders, marketing, store activity, and Gold output retain their related contracts. Reusable intermediate data is cached where repeated calculations would otherwise recompute it.

Execution performance¶

Generation is a dependency graph of stages (STAGES in generation/engine.py). Each stage names its inputs, its outputs, and the setup notebook that runs it:

Notebook Stages Waits for
setup-03-generate-sales candidate receipts, fulfillment plan setup-04
setup-04-generate-online-orders online orders setup-01
setup-05-generate-marketing background impressions setup-01
setup-06-generate-returns-and-promotions returns, promotions setup-03
setup-07-generate-store-activity store operations, foot traffic, BLE setup-03
setup-08-generate-inventory inventory, logistics, and stockout ledgers setup-03
setup-09-attribute-marketing last-touch attribution setup-03 to 06
setup-10-publish-silver invariants and atomic Silver publication setup-02 to 04, 06 to 09

setup-pipeline follows the same dependency graph at notebook level; a test checks that the two graphs agree. Independent notebooks run in separate Spark sessions. At most five run at once, within the session concurrency that capacity sizing reserves for required ML. Setup-10 runs its invariant checks and per-table staging, validation, and promotion concurrently; every phase still completes for all tables before the next starts, and any promotion failure rolls back every table.

Gold (setup-11-build-gold) runs in the first ml-required wave with the demand forecast, market basket, promotion effectiveness and stockout models, before customer segmentation and churn, so the pipeline needs at most five sessions. Setup-11 holds the gold writer lock in ag._job_locks while it publishes. Inside a notebook, up to six ready stages run concurrently. A stage writes its own outputs one after another so related cached plans are never built concurrently. Completion order and the notebook split do not change the output; a test compares the notebook path with single-session generate_all row for row.

Stage outputs that a later notebook needs are written as gen__<stage>__<output> Delta tables in the Silver staging schema. Each records the generation fingerprint (a hash of the generation configuration and of the dictionary files generation reads) in its Delta commit, and loading a handoff produced for another configuration or other dictionary content fails. Setup-10 promotes single-handoff facts directly from their handoffs and drops every handoff only after publication succeeds, so a failed publication can be retried without regenerating. Within a notebook, other stage frames are checkpointed under Files/setup/_checkpoints/<notebook> in the Lakehouse rather than on executors. Later steps therefore do not depend on an executor's local data surviving autoscaling or executor loss. generate_all (local tests) runs the whole graph in one session with the same checkpoints, which stay on the executors when no folder is given.

Generation output never depends on Spark partitioning, so each notebook sizes spark.sql.shuffle.partitions to the configured receipt-line volume (about one million lines per partition, between 200 and 8,192) and lets adaptive execution coalesce small shuffles. Materializing each stage before dependants consume it also means later plans start from small leaves instead of re-analyzing the full upstream lineage on the driver. The inventory policy runs in Python workers through mapInArrow, which moves demand and events in Arrow column batches rather than pickling each row. BLE zone selection, ping sequencing, and zone changes are per-visit array operations, so the ping stream is never shuffled.

Retail behavior and calibration¶

Historical store demand starts with open-hour visitors. A seeded negative-binomial count controls arrival variability; each visitor has a purchase decision using store/hour conversion propensity. Footfall uses this same visitor grid, not receipts divided by a fixed conversion percentage, and counts zone passages along the shared visit path (see Store profiles and derived defaults).

Visitors per store-day also follow the US holiday calendar and the local weather (generation/stream_calendar.py, shared with the live stream):

  • Holidays. holiday_dates lists New Year's Day, Presidents' Day, Valentine's Day, Easter Saturday and Sunday, Mother's Day, Memorial Day and the Saturday before it, Father's Day, July 3 and 4, Labor Day, Halloween, Thanksgiving eve and day, Black Friday and the Saturday after, Cyber Monday, Christmas Eve, Christmas, December 26, and New Year's Eve. Each holiday scales the day's traffic by the store type's class (store_class: general, grocery, hardware, or luxury, from the department mix and ticket size) and online orders by its own factor (HOLIDAY_EFFECTS). A factor of 0 closes the stores: Christmas for every class, Thanksgiving for all but grocers. Closed days have no receipts, store operations, or returns, and returns due on one move to the next day.
  • Weather. Each geography has a daily weather index: a weighted sum of the last 8 days' seeded shocks (weights 0.7 to the power of the age), so weather persists for days (lag-1 autocorrelation about 0.7). Its effect on traffic depends on the state's climate and the month (snow-belt winters, southern storm summers), within 0.4-1.2x and about 1 on average. An index of 2 or more marks a severe-weather day (severe_weather_column); the logistics incident model opens WEATHER incidents on those days.

Customer selection has persistent, long-tailed visit propensities and geography affinity. Continuous customer spending propensity and quick, regular, family, and bulk shopping missions shape basket sizes. Most lines share the trip's department preference; others permit cross-department shopping. Product popularity is persistent, price-sensitive, and uneven. Catalog selection excludes products not yet launched.

avg_ticket_target calibrates expected line counts against actual eligible catalog prices, category quantities, tax, and expected discounts. basket_lambda limits the nominal mean line count; neither setting changes catalog prices or forces every ticket to the target. One-line minimums and expensive catalogs can make a target unattainable. A target below the cost of one line produces only single-item receipts, so each store type's target must allow multi-item baskets: required market-basket recommendations depend on them. Report distributions and calibration error rather than truncating individual tickets. Consumables and durables use different geometric quantity distributions, with single units most common and no artificial four-unit maximum.

Promotion exposure precedes basket-size sampling and raises additional-item demand. Discounts still precede tax, and receipt/payment amounts reconcile in integer cents. Price response comes from the shelf-price schedule and the department elasticities, and product pairs from fixed complements (see Prices, baskets and promotions); both are synthetic designs, not models learned from real transactions.

The shared draw implementation uses Box-Muller normal draws and an exact integer-shape negative-binomial distribution (sum of geometric draws). All decisions retain stable key/seed salts, independent of Spark partitioning. The changed simulation intentionally produces different data from older code with the same seed; reproducibility requires the same code, dictionaries, configuration, and date window.

Dimensions depend only on the seed, the scale settings, and anchor_date, never on the run date. Product launch dates are placed around anchor_date, which retail-setup configure pins once per environment and render passes to the setup notebooks (it defaults to start_date). Rendering the same months configuration on a later day therefore regenerates identical stores, geographies, customers, and products; only the fact window moves. Each department's first product still launches before the history starts, even when the history begins before the anchor. Floating-point catalog weights are summed in product order, so they are bit-identical regardless of how Spark delivers rows.

Inventory feedback and unfulfilled demand¶

The replenishment policy covers every selling store/SKU. It learns from past attempted demand, considers on-hand and pending deliveries/accepted restocks, and orders case-pack quantities against coverage and safety-stock targets. Each SKU starts from a prior of its expected daily demand at the first history day (store volume and multiplier, lines per basket, seasonal department weight, the SKU's popularity share of its launched department, units per line): the prior starts the demand estimate and sizes opening stock between the reorder point and the order-up-to target (4-8 days of cover). It never looks at generated demand, and it removes the start-of-history stockout wave a flat opening stock caused.

Each DC simulates one product for all of its stores at once (dc_supply.simulate_dc_sku), so DC stock is shared. Every SKU a store reorders when a day closes ships on its next open delivery slot as one load, which drives the lane and is received when unloading starts, 15 minutes after arrival. The DC ships the day's reorders from the stock it has, most urgent first; a reorder it cannot cover is short-shipped, so the store reorders again the next day and runs out if the DC stays empty. inventory.py packs each load into shipments of at most truck_capacity units in product order (_split_reorders_by_capacity; IDs SHP-<dispatch yyyyMMdd>-<store>-<leg>), splitting a SKU's receipt across legs when needed. Shipments go, in loading order, to a truck that is free for the whole trip (loading until it is back at the DC): the store's route truck for the first leg, the shared pool and then the DC's other trucks for overflow legs. Dwell is the unload model shared with the live stream (network.unload_minutes: base minutes plus units at the site's rate, slower in the store's busy hours and quicker at night, plus a door wait for a truck 30 or more minutes late; 30-180 minutes). Each load has a planned and an actual schedule (incidents.plan_delivery): moves before arrival show the plan in eta/etd with delay_minutes 0; from ARRIVED on they show the actual arrival, delay_minutes (actual minus planned_arrival_time) and delay_reason.

A candidate line is fulfilled in full only when its stock is available. Rejected lines are omitted from realized receipts; empty receipts disappear. Receipt totals, discounts, tax, payments, and refunds are reconciled against fulfilled lines. The return plan preserves sampled dates and identities while only actual fulfilled/refunded units can be restocked. Dependent promotions and attribution consume realized sales, not attempted demand.

Returns and payments¶

About return_rate of sales are returned 1-14 days later, at a random time inside the store's hours (store-local), favouring baskets whose categories customers return more (returns.RETURN_CATEGORY_WEIGHTS: apparel and electronics far more than fresh food). A return is partial: each line of the receipt is returned with a probability that follows its category (at least one line), and a multi-unit line returns some or all of its units. The refund keeps the sale's customer_id, goes back to the tender that paid for the sale, and links it in fact_receipts.original_receipt_id_ext (NULL on sales). Refund cents are the returned share of each line's net with integer basis-point tax; return lines keep their own promo_code (receipt_type = RETURN marks them). Only returned units the store filled can be restocked.

Payments follow the shared decline model (vocabulary.payment_outcome_column): the first tender declines with its probability; 80% of declined shoppers retry on another tender (a DECLINED then an APPROVED payment), the rest walk away and the sale is voided, so it never becomes a receipt and takes no stock. A voided online order is cancelled (zero totals, no payment). Every receipt and placed order therefore has exactly one APPROVED payment, and its header tender is the approved one. Transaction IDs derive from the purchase ID and attempt (TXN-<receipt or order>-<attempt>), so they are unique.

fact_store_inventory_txn appends two long audit columns after the existing schema: requested_quantity and unfulfilled_quantity. LOST_SALE rows record rejected demand with zero physical quantity; they must not decrement stock. Store/DC/truck transfers share the fulfillment plan, and inventory positions exclude deliveries outside the requested history. Physical-stock invariants verify nonnegative balances, running-sum conservation, opening stock, receipt/refund mirrors, demand accounting, and transfer conservation.

Streaming replay preserves historical demand-audit fields. Older ledgers without these columns derive fulfilled requested quantities from negative SALE movements and use zero unfulfilled demand, because the older producer did not record lost sales. This is read-side compatibility only: writes still require the declared target schema. Regenerate historical setup or explicitly migrate the target before resuming streaming against an older table. Live sales carry requested_quantity; a live line the store cannot fill is a LOST_SALE with its demand in unfulfilled_quantity, as in history.

DCs keep their own stock (fact_dc_inventory_txn): an opening stock of 10-16 days of their stores' expected demand, supplier orders when the position (on hand plus open orders) falls to the reorder point (lead time + 1 + 4 safety days of smoothed demand, at least a case per store), received a stable 3-7 day lead time later at 04:00 DC time (INBOUND_SHIPMENT, short during a supplier short-ship), truck loads (OUTBOUND_SHIPMENT), and ship-from-DC online lines when they ship (ONLINE_SHIPMENT; units the DC lacks are EXPEDITED_RECEIPT just before). A DC stockout is a fact_stockouts row with DCID. The model does not optimize fleet routing. Overflow legs can only overlap a truck's earlier trip when they outnumber the shared pool. Historical online orders keep their own fulfillment statuses; do not read them as reservations against the store policy.

Logistics incidents¶

incidents.plan_incidents lays out every disruption from the seed and the dates, the same way for history and the live stream:

Type Frequency Effect
DC_OUTAGE 1.5 per DC and year, 6-36 hours no dispatches; loads leave at each store's next slot after it
LANE_DELAY 4 per DC and year, 2-12 hours, one store trucks on the lane arrive 45-180 minutes late
WEATHER severe-weather days, more often in the climate's rough season, 1-4 days lanes into the geography's stores run 60-240 minutes late
HOLIDAY_CAPACITY Black Friday weekend and the days after Christmas, 70% of DCs loading starts 60-240 minutes late
SUPPLIER_SHORT_SHIP 2 per DC and year, 3-10 days, one supplier group (a sixth of products) 20-60% of supplier orders arrive

Severity follows the delay (90/150 minutes), outage length (12/24 hours) or lost supply share (50/65%). Loads never arrive on a store's closure day, and traffic delays some runs (5-20% by lane length, more on Fridays) by 15-50% of the lane time. delay_reason names the largest cause of a delay (DC_OUTAGE, HOLIDAY_CAPACITY, LANE_DELAY, WEATHER, TRAFFIC); the live stream adds TRUCK_AVAILABILITY when a load waited for a free truck. Frequencies and ranges live in generation/calibration.py.

End-of-history incident. Every history also ends inside one prolonged DC_OUTAGE (incidents.end_of_history_incident), keyed only on the seed and the history's last day. The DC serving the most stores (a stable draw breaks ties) stops dispatching between 00:00 and 05:59 DC time two days before the last day, for 78-96 hours (END_OF_HISTORY_OUTAGE_*; longer than the random 6-36 hours on purpose, HIGH severity). Its cause is a warehouse system outage or a labor shortage. Its ID is the random outage ID with an -END suffix, for example INC-OUT-001-20250815-END. The DC's stores get no loads for the last three days, so their fast movers (about two units a day or more) really run low: at the as-of, some of them have under three days of cover, and some are empty. Slow movers keep at least a case (12 units) on the shelf and stay well covered. The incident lands in fact_logistics_incidents and fact_logistics_incident_stores with a NULL resolved_at. The live stream plans the same outage from END_DATE, starts it in its first batch and emits logistics_incident_resolved at its planned end, 6-54 hours after the last local day. Random incidents are unchanged.

Historical/live boundary¶

The stream notebook embeds the shared basket/catalog/quantity code at build time. It emits a variable-length receipt-line array with matching item counts, promotion products, line discounts, tax, header totals, and payments. Live online orders use history's online promo codes, tenders, declines and destination tax. Missing store tax or an empty/missing eligible catalog is an error, not a synthetic $9.99 price.

Live catalog launch eligibility is snapshotted when the stream starts; restart the stream to pick up newly launched products or changed catalog/profile data. Live store visits continue the historical visitor-arrival process: the same hourly, daily and monthly weights in store-local time, the same traffic multipliers and operating hours, the same conversion model, and customers from the same ticket-weighted local pools (see the live event contract). Live store stock, trucks, DC stock, incidents and online order lifecycles are state machines that continue the history (see the live event contract); their state lives in ag._stream_state_*. A new stream incarnation reads store stock from the Silver ledger as of its start.

Live fact_store_ops rows use the historical lower-case opened/closed; notebook 03 lower-cases rows written by earlier stream versions. Live fact_marketing journey touches are dated by when they were shown (impression_time), not when they arrived, and live fact_promotions.product_ids is the same comma-separated, numerically sorted list as history.

Marketing and attribution¶

Background impressions cost real CPMs: $0.002-$0.03 an impression by channel and device (vocabulary.CHANNEL_COSTS), with cost to four decimals and cost_cents rounded at random so summed spend is unbiased. Each campaign rotates a small creative pool per channel (CRT<campaign>-<channel>-<n>, four creatives). Conversion follows exposure (attribution.py): an impression reaches the customer of its ad ID and converts with a small probability weighted by channel (search and email best, display worst) when that customer makes an eligible purchase within seven days; the probability is calibrated per run so the campaigns return about TARGET_ROAS (4x) their spend, which keeps conversions well under 1% of impressions. A journey is the converting impressions themselves, so its touches look like every other impression (same ID and creative formats, CRM-matched customer_id at the same rate) except for attribution_journey_id; every campaign family can convert, and each touch sits in its own campaign window. Last touch decides the campaign. The live stream attributes the share of eligible purchases that returns the same ROAS on its spend (attribution.live_journey_rate), with one to three touches built like background impressions.

Statistical acceptance¶

Use multiple seeds and short and multi-year windows. Check ticket and basket percentiles, customer purchase-frequency dispersion, SKU concentration, conversion variability, stockout duration/recovery, inventory conservation, and historical/live basket distributions. Keep legitimate price points, integer quantities, business constraints, and calibrated model-score plateaus; do not add jitter to hide them. Local synthetic calibration targets are demo assumptions, not claims about measured industry distributions.

Invariants¶

Current invariant checks include:

  • key uniqueness and required foreign keys;
  • non-null event dates;
  • online-order header/line integrity;
  • pricing, tax, and promotion consistency;
  • seven-day last-touch uniqueness and impression/purchase linkage;
  • attributed revenue, discount, tax, total, and payment reconciliation;
  • stockout location exclusivity;
  • physical stock, transfer conservation, and explicit unfulfilled-demand accounting;
  • truck timing and inventory relationships;
  • sales only within each store's operating hours, evaluated in store-local time, and local_hour equal to the store-local hour of event_ts;
  • returns linked to their sale (same store and customer), posted after it (on a later store-local day, by construction), refunding no more than the sale;
  • exactly one approved payment per receipt and order, unique transaction IDs;
  • logistics incidents reference known DCs and stores and resolve after they start;
  • no receipt line sold before its product's launch date;
  • returns posted strictly after the originating sale (never same-day);
  • product lifecycle dates present (no degenerate lifecycles).

utility/src/retail_setup/generation/invariants.py enforces the shared live/batch invariants: operating hours, product launch eligibility, return timing, lifecycle presence, and validated profile controls.

Naming compatibility¶

New columns use snake_case, but current TMDL compatibility requires explicit legacy exceptions such as ID, StoreNumber, CustomerId, StoreID, ReceiptId, Source, Subtotal, and __index_level_0__. These exceptions are documented in schemas.py and must not be silently normalized in a transform.

Store profiles and derived defaults¶

Supported profiles are supercenter, grocery, hardware, and luxury, plus per-environment custom business layouts (custom-<slug>, see the setup CLI customize contract). Custom layouts are built deterministically from a saved spec by the same catalog builder as the preset trees (retail_setup.dictionaries.catalog). Their products.json rows add explicit Taxability and Refrigerated values, which take precedence over the department/category name rules preset catalogs rely on. Their brand Category equals a department name, and branded titles use a brand from the product's own department.

A custom layout's department_weights are calibrated when its dictionaries are built (retail_setup.customization.calibrate). Copilot tends to answer with revenue-like shares, which, read as basket-line shares, leave a realistic ticket target buying about one item. When the ticket target affords fewer than min(basket_lambda, 2) items, the weights move toward line share (weight * expected_line_value ** -alpha) by the smallest alpha in [0, 1] that reaches it, or alpha = 1 when nothing smaller does. The ticket target and catalog are unchanged, the saved spec keeps Copilot's weights, and profile.json carries the calibrated ones, so batch, stream and estimates all use them. Dictionaries built by an older calibration are rebuilt automatically. Current derived defaults include:

  • silver_db = ag
  • gold_db = au
  • dc_count = max(1, store_count // 10)
  • customer_count = max(store_count * transactions_per_store_day * 365 * 1.0525 * (1 - anonymous_share) / (0.75 * v), 5000), where v is the profile's customer_visits_per_year (supercenter 8, grocery 9, hardware 6, luxury 2.5): about 14,000 customers per supercenter
  • anonymous_share = 0.45
  • annual_churn_rate = 0.20
  • annual_volume_growth = 0.03
  • transactions_per_store_day = the store type's profile value (see below)
  • online_orders_per_day = store_count * transactions_per_store_day * 1.0525 * s / (1 - s), where s is the profile's online_order_share and 1.0525 is the mean store traffic multiplier, so online orders are s of all orders
  • return_rate = 0.01
  • brands_per_product = 3
  • truck_capacity = 15000

Each store type's profile sets its store knobs. Presets declare them; custom layouts derive them:

Store type Receipts per store-day Operating hours Store formats Online share
supercenter 400 6-22, 7-23, 24h hypermarket, superstore, standard 12%
grocery 280 6-22, 7-22, 7-23 superstore, standard, neighborhood, express 15%
hardware 180 6-21, 7-21, 7-22 superstore, standard, neighborhood 20%
luxury 40 10-19, 10-20, 11-19 boutique 25%
custom 400 * (85 / avg_ticket_target) ** 0.6, 20-1000 hours whose hourly weight reaches 10% of the peak (24h when that is all day) boutique at a $500+ ticket, else all other formats profile value

A store's format is drawn from those its volume class suits among the store type's formats. Every store follows its store type's operating_hours patterns ("24h" or "open-close"). Foot traffic, BLE pings and zone changes use the store type's zones (upper-cased), in history and in the live stream, and the same visit path (vocabulary.visit_zone_path): entrance, one to three department zones in random order, checkout (buyers) and exit.

fact_foot_traffic counts zone passages per store-hour, not shares of the visitors: the entrance and exit count every visitor of the hour, checkout counts the hour's buyers (so it is never below the receipts), and each department zone the visitors who browse it (on average two zones a visitor). Visitor totals and conversion use the entrance count; the semantic model's Foot Traffic Count does so unless a zone is filtered. Beacons detect a quarter of visits (BLE_DETECTED_SHARE), buyers and non-buyers alike. A detected buyer's pings run entrance, departments, checkout and exit, with the purchase in the middle of its checkout dwell; a non-buyer starts inside the hour and never passes checkout. Pings and zone changes stay inside store hours.

Customers and churn¶

generation/customers.py gives every customer a lifecycle, as pure functions of the seed and their ID, shared by history and the live stream:

  • Home store. dim_customers.HomeStoreID is drawn in proportion to store traffic, and the customer lives in that store's geography. 85% of a customer's identified visits are at the home store; the rest are at another store, picked by traffic.
  • Anonymous shoppers. About anonymous_share (45%) of in-store receipts have no customer_id (cash and non-loyalty shoppers). Returns, BLE pings of anonymous visits and most marketing impressions also carry none. Every consumer filters or tolerates NULL customer_id.
  • Visit frequency. Each customer's yearly trip rate is lognormal around the profile's customer_visits_per_year, capped at three trips a week (156 a year), and replicated as selection tickets.
  • Acquisition. Customers who already existed when history starts are active from the first day; the rest sign up uniformly across the history, so first purchases keep arriving and the base turns over. The two groups are sized so acquisitions replace churn.
  • Churn. A customer's lifetime is exponential with a hazard driven by segment (budget and convenience shoppers churn sooner than brand-loyal and quality shoppers), trip frequency (infrequent visitors churn sooner) and basket budget (small spenders churn sooner). The base hazard is calibrated so annual_churn_rate (default 20%) of the customers active at a time churn within a year. 75% of churners taper: their visits fall linearly to 15% of their rate over the 60 days before they stop, the early-warning signal a churn model can learn. The rest stop abruptly.
  • Online orders and marketing. An online order's customer is active on the order day (the first of four tries; an order that finds none is not placed), and a CRM-matched impression's customer is signed up and not churned.

A receipt is identified with a calibrated probability, picks a ticket from its pool, and keeps that customer with their activity on the receipt's local date (1 while active, tapering before churn, 0 before signup or after churn); otherwise it is anonymous. Churn dates are not published: models learn them from purchase behavior.

Prices, baskets and promotions¶

generation/pricing.py sets every SKU's shelf price per week, as a pure function of the seed, the SKU and the date, shared by receipts, online orders and the live stream:

  • dim_products.SalePrice is the list price at anchor_date. Regular prices follow a per-department inflation trend from there (for example grocery 3%, electronics -3% a year).
  • Every 13 weeks (each SKU on its own phase) a price review resets the regular price within 6% of trend; 30% of review periods carry a one-to-three week markdown of 10-35%. Regular prices end in .49 or .99 (9 cents below $1); markdown prices end in 9 cents and always undercut the regular price.
  • fact_receipt_lines.unit_cents and fact_online_order_lines.unit_cents are the shelf price on the sale's local date. No separate price-history table is published: reports read sale prices from the lines and catalog prices from dim_products.
  • Product choice responds to price: within a department, a SKU's choice weight scales with (price / trend price) ** elasticity, with department elasticities from about -0.8 (groceries, jewelry) to -2.5 (electronics), so markdowns sell more units.
  • Store traffic and online orders grow annual_volume_growth (3%) a year from the anchor date.

Store baskets (generation/baskets.py, shared with the stream) are built per receipt:

  1. Lines are drawn by department missions as before; 15% of lines bring along their complementary product, a fixed partner in the same department, so market-basket analysis finds real pairs.
  2. A SKU drawn twice becomes one line with the summed quantity.
  3. A receipt with an offer (the profile's promo_rate) gets one promotion code for the whole basket, drawn among the PROMO_CATALOG codes in season whose minimum (for example SAVE20 at $50, BFRIDAY40 at $100) the basket gross subtotal meets. Seasonal codes are three times as likely while in season. Each code discounts only the lines in its department scope (for example BACKTOSCHOOL20 on clothing, office and electronics); BOGO50 discounts every second unit of eligible lines with two or more units. A line carries at most one code, and fact_promotions has at most one row per receipt.
  4. Tax is integer basis-point math on each line's net amount, at the share of the store's combined rate its state's taxability rule sets (_shared/tax_rules.json, baskets.line_tax_cents). Taxable goods pay the full rate everywhere. Grocery staples (NON_TAXABLE) are exempt unless the state taxes food (for example Alabama, Mississippi, South Dakota, Tennessee at a reduced share). Clothing (REDUCED_RATE) pays the full rate unless the state exempts it (Minnesota, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Vermont) or exempts items under a price (New York under $110, Massachusetts under $175, Rhode Island under $250). Online orders apply the fulfilling store's state, or the customer's for ship-from-DC orders; returns refund at the original rule. Dictionary sets without the rules file use the defaults (groceries exempt, clothing taxed).

Fulfillment prices the basket again over the lines the store could fill, so a promotion whose minimum the filled basket no longer meets is replaced or dropped. The invariants check one code per receipt, met minimums, and no repeated SKU within a receipt.

Removed active-path behavior¶

Local FastAPI control, DuckDB persistence, parquet export, Blob upload, Event Hubs, outbox, dead-letter queue (DLQ), and Prometheus services are not part of the supported Fabric-native contract.
